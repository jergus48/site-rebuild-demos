What the Commission does

Six lines of work, one purpose.

CLAIMANTSFILE 01

Support for claimants

Guidance, expertise and assistance for claimants worldwide, including identifying, locating and recovering looted property on their behalf.

INSTITUTIONSFILE 02

Working with institutions

With museums, governments and other institutions to identify and locate looted cultural property and ensure its return.

CLAIMSFILE 03

Restitution claims

Supporting and pursuing restitution claims and procedures in all countries.

POLICYFILE 04

Policy and legislation

Promoting public policy and legislative change throughout Europe so these issues can be resolved justly.

ART TRADEFILE 05

Codes of practice

Codes of practice for auction houses and the art trade, particularly on provenance information and essential records.

ADRFILE 06

Alternative dispute resolution

Promoting alternative dispute resolution mechanisms for looted cultural property cases.

Monitoring and furthering the international principles for dealing with looted cultural property and its restitution.

About

Working since 1999.

The Commission for Looted Art in Europe was founded in 1999. It shares examples of its cases, news of its work and the international principles it monitors and promotes.

Mandate

The Commission is mandated to represent the European Council of Jewish Communities (ECJC) and the Conference of European Rabbis (CER).
